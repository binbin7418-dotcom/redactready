## One-liner

Black boxes drawn over text are not redaction — the text is still in the file. RedactReady removes the content itself, in batch, then searches every output twice and only passes files with zero leaks.

## The problem

Law firms, clinics, agencies and sellers redact PDFs every week: names, case numbers, emails, IDs. The most common failure in the wild is a black rectangle drawn *over* live text — anyone can copy-paste the "redacted" secret back out, and it happens in real court filings and FOIA releases every year. Free tools do exactly that, and nothing checks the result.

## What it does

- Batch: drop in PDFs or a folder; list the terms to remove (names, case numbers, IDs — one per line)
- Optional pattern redaction: email addresses and US SSN-shaped numbers
- **Real redaction:** matched text is deleted from the PDF content stream and the area painted black (PyMuPDF redaction annotations + apply) — not covered, *removed*
- **Two-channel leak re-check on every output:**
  1. the extracted-text layer is searched for every term
  2. the **raw content-stream bytes** of every page are searched, plain and hex-encoded — because text can hide where extractors don't look
- A file only PASSes with zero hits in both channels
- **Scan honesty:** if a page has no text layer (a scanned image), the file is flagged SCAN-DETECTED and never passed silently — you get told it needs OCR, not a false sense of security
- Originals never modified; outputs and a full report go to a new folder

## Honest limits

- Text-based PDFs only; scanned/image pages are flagged, not processed (OCR first, then redact)
- A term rendered as vector outlines or baked into an image is out of reach of any text redactor — the scan flag and the report exist so you never *assume* coverage you don't have
- Redaction is permanent in the outputs — that's the point; keep your originals (the tool never touches them)
